# Practical UI applied to the Portfolio

Original implementation notes adapted from JJ's approved study guide for Adham Dannaway's _Practical UI_, second edition (2024). References use PDF page numbers; the study report checked printed body numbers on inspected pages. This is targeted guidance, not a reproduction or substitute for the book.

**Provisional source coverage:** the first pass consulted prose across 373 pages through local OCR and closely reviewed 48 visual pages. It did not inspect every diagram or complete the tutorials. Revised visual coverage and reconciliation are pending; do not represent this adaptation as a completed visual study. These notes have not independently checked the PDF or current accessibility standards.

## Task and hierarchy

Choose a clear reading order: what this page is, why its content matters, and where a visitor goes next. Group related material with proximity and alignment before adding framing. Keep the important navigation and authoring prerequisites visible. Use a restrained type/spacing hierarchy instead of giving every section equal emphasis. References: Fundamentals, pp. 17–25, 40–46; Less is more, pp. 56–71; Layout and spacing, pp. 164–188.

JJ's simple editorial direction is his preference, not a rule prescribed by the book. Preserve the approved portrait identity and image-led work cards. Use cards for distinct projects and links; avoid extra containers around ordinary article paragraphs. Reuse existing component behavior as well as appearance. References: Fundamentals, pp. 26–34, 44–47; Colour, pp. 109–112, 146–151.

## Layout and reading

Keep a consistent text edge, smaller gaps inside a group and larger gaps between groups. Adapt columns and gutters to width; let long titles and descriptions wrap without clipping needed meaning. Preserve intrinsic image dimensions and inspect the actual responsive crop. Choose readable line length and line height in the existing system fonts, rather than transplanting a book example's dimensions. References: Layout and spacing, pp. 189–216; Typography, pp. 230–256.

## Color and controls

Use semantic roles for text, surfaces, boundaries, actions and status. Check actual foreground/background combinations in light and dark themes, including focus, hover, disabled states and image overlays. Provide cues beyond color, clear action labels, comfortable hit areas and spacing. Explain prerequisites and failures where the author can act on them; hiding a field must not leave an invisible validation blocker. References: Colour, pp. 79–90, 96–113, 116–151; Buttons, pp. 296–324; Forms, pp. 331–368.

## Copy and authoring

Keep headings and links meaningful when scanned. Reduce repeated explanations while retaining facts and qualifications. Keep labels visible after typing, show essential hints before input and make error recovery specific. Keep placement checkboxes independent and consistent with their saved behavior. References: Copywriting, pp. 267–287; Forms, pp. 331–368.

## Verification limits

The screenshot and interaction checks in this repository are an original Codex workflow, not a testing procedure attributed to the book. Follow the project's applicable accessibility requirements; source recommendations are starting points, not compliance certification. The book's 2024 discussion does not establish current standards and excludes front-end screen-reader implementation (pp. 35–39, 79–84). Consult the private source for precise examples when available; report uncertainty when it is not. Keep the PDF, images and OCR private and outside the repository.
